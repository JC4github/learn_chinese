# Beginner learning paths

The library contains 14 original lessons: the four initial graded stories plus the ten additions below. Every Chinese vocabulary token, guided listening example and complete story has bundled Mandarin audio. The content is authored for this project; the sources below were used to check general pronunciation guidance, not to copy lesson text.

| Path | Lesson | Practice goal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pinyin | One Sound, Four Tones | mā / má / mǎ / mà; tone marks and pitch |
| Pinyin | Buying or Selling? | mǎi / mài; third versus fourth tone |
| Pinyin | Four, Not Ten | sì / shí; s versus sh and tone contrast |
| Pinyin | A Little Question, a Light Tone | Neutral tone, light syllables, and 你好 tone change |
| Conversation | Nice to Meet You | Introductions and asking a name |
| Conversation | Ordering a Drink | Ordering, hot/iced choice, and price |
| Conversation | At the Checkout | Bags and card payment |
| Conversation | Finding the Station | Polite questions and basic directions |
| Conversation | Making Plans for Tomorrow | Invitations, time and place |
| Conversation | Could You Say That Again? | Asking for repetition and slower speech |

Pinyin and Conversations are available as library filters, personal interests and home-page shortcuts. Pinyin appears on the guided practice buttons even when it is hidden in the reading text. Each dialogue has labelled turns, optional translations, and a comprehension question. Listening and repeating aloud is self-practice; the app does not score microphone recordings.

Written pinyin includes common spoken tone changes for 一 and 不 in the new dialogue. 你好 retains the familiar nǐ hǎo spelling and its coaching note explains the third-tone change heard in speech. A third tone is described as low, with the full dipping/rising form when pronounced in isolation; connected speech differs.

Reference checks: [MIT Hanyu Pinyin: Tones](https://web.mit.edu/jinzhang/www/pinyin/tones/), [Open University: Getting Started with Chinese — Pinyin and Tones](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/mod/oucontent/view.php?id=106437&section=3), and [Chinese Language Institute: Pinyin chart](https://studycli.org/pinyin-chart/).

The audio is synthetic. Native-speaker review remains a useful editorial check for pronunciation, names and natural teaching dialogue.
